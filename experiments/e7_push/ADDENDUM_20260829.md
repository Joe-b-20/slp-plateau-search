## B.1 The finding, verbatim from `ATLAS_NOTE.md`

> Three of the 23 published `ctl=OK … exhaustive: no in-sector program at
> `|B|-1`` verdicts in `atlas/thinktank/basin/b9b_*.log` are **vacuous**: in
> those three instances the in-sector optimum is **strictly greater than
> `|B|`**, so the known-achievable length `|B|` is itself unreachable in the
> space being searched. The `|B|-1` negative therefore has no positive control
> behind it and carries no information about 87 — it reports that the *scoped
> space is too poor to reproduce the circuit that already exists*, not that the
> block is irreducible.

| block | `\|B\|` | in-sector optimum |
|---|---|---|
| 88@5 S0 | 8 | **exactly 9** (no program at ≤ 8; verified 9-gate program) |
| 88@7 S0 | 9 | **exactly 10** (no program at ≤ 9; verified 10-gate program) |
| 88@8 S0 | 9 | **exactly 10** (no program at ≤ 9; witness decoded at 10) |

> All three are exactly `|B| + 1`: the sector-0 block is *one gate more
> expensive in-sector than in the circuit*, which is precisely the price of the
> descendant-operand the block reads and the instance cannot supply.

**Cause.** `b9b_sweep.control(n,k)` counts an operand as a problem only when it
lies outside the 8-dimensional sector span, while `b9_blockopt.block()` builds
`AVAIL` by excluding the block **and everything downstream of it**. The block is
**not topologically convex**: a gate `o` can be simultaneously a descendant of
one block gate and an operand of another. `o` is excluded from `AVAIL` by
`desc` while being perfectly in-sector, so the control prints `ctl=OK`.
Counted over all 112 blocks, **18 have at least one such operand, 10 of them in
sector 0**; only 3 of the 18 ever reached a published `ctl=OK` negative, and
**no other verdict in the sweep is affected**.

## B.2 The withdrawal text, verbatim

> 1. The three `b9b_0.log` lines above must be withdrawn as **vacuous
>    negatives**: the sentence "no in-sector program at `|B|-1`" is *true* but
>    says nothing, because there is none at `|B|` or at any length. They must
>    not be counted as sector-0 blocks that were checked for an 87.
> 2. §6.2's claim that the free structural control "is doing real work — it
>    immediately shows that tap-sector blocks are genuinely entangled with
>    out-of-sector values while clean-sector blocks are self-contained" needs
>    one qualification: the control is **sufficient only for topologically
>    convex blocks**. It cannot see a descendant-operand, and sector-0 blocks
>    systematically have one. The entanglement law itself is unaffected (it is
>    about sectors 1/3/4 and is confirmed by 35 independent `NOT-COVERED`
>    lines).
> 3. §9's status line for §6.2 should note that sector 0 was **mis-specified**,
>    not merely unfinished: 58 of 112 blocks were printed, 23 negatives claimed,
>    3 of them void.

## B.3 The repair, and the fact that it closes sector 0

Replace the block by its topologically convex (interval) closure
`B* = B ∪ { g : g is a descendant of B and an ancestor of B }` and rebuild
`NEEDED` / `AVAIL` from `B*`. `ATLAS_NOTE.md` gives the two-line proof that
after the repair no outside operand of `B*` is a descendant of `B*`, so the
existing coverage test becomes sufficient.

The e7 ledger confirms the repair ran and closed the sector — 19
`convex_block` rows, 3 `CONTROL_FAILED_no_program_at_nB` (the three vacuous
blocks, `lower_bound_proven` 9/10/10) and **15 `OPTIMAL_AT_nB` rows covering all
14 sector-0 blocks** (88@5 twice), every one `min_gates == nB == lower_bound_proven`,
**1,314.5 s total**:

|---|---|

| p88_00 9 · p88_01 9 · p88_02 9 · p88_03 6 · p88_04 10 · p88_05 9 · p88_06 8 · p88_07 8 | |

> **With the repair, sector 0 is not merely askable, it is closed: 14/14 blocks
